\paragraph{In-distribution matching of ensemble statistics} We begin by looking at how
the SR samples generated by the baseline model match ensemble spectral statistics of the
flow at its home regime, i.e. $Re=1000$. The results obtained in Figure
\ref{fig:ensemble-E(k)-re-1000} replicate the findings of \cite{shu2023physics}. Figure
\ref{fig:ensemble-ratio-re-1000} details that the base model generations on-average
agree with the ground truth energy spectrum in the lower wavenumber $k$ range, as
$\nicefrac{E(k)}{E_{GT}(k)}\approx1$ up to $k\approx7$. The model then under-energises
parts of the flow with modes $k\geq7$, as $\nicefrac{E(k)}{E_{GT}(k)}=0.4$ by $k=50$ and
never recovers. Across the $k\in[16,96)$ range, the base model retains only $66\%$ of
the energy content of the ground truth flow, while its residual reaches only
$r_{x_0}=0.63\times r_{GT}$, confirming the over-smoothing, at an MSE of $0.40$. This
result perfectly illustrates the fact that models trained on an L2 objective
$\|\epsilon_\theta^t - \epsilon^t\|_2^2$ have a tendency to smooth out small-scale
turbulence in the snapshots, in turn generating visually correct but energetically
incorrect and physically too-smooth super-resolved snapshots compared to the ground
truth.
