Does residual conditioning survive out of distribution?

Five reconstruction models — a plain diffusion base and four residual-conditioned adapters, all trained at Re=1000 — tested on Kolmogorov flow at Re 500, 1000, and 2000. Each conditional model is fed the Navier–Stokes residual evaluated at the target Reynolds number. The question: does the learned physics correction still help when the flow is out of distribution?

The two findings

grad_frozen60 · the reliable one

−5.5%

The faithful Shu et al. adapter (gradient signal, frozen base) lowers the PDE residual by a near-constant −5.8 / −5.4 / −5.3% across Re 500 / 1000 / 2000 — Reynolds-number-agnostic. It generalises.

field_full60 · the OOD one

−9.7%

The ENS-style raw-residual signal with full fine-tuning barely moves in-distribution, but its benefit grows with turbulence: −0.4% → −4.1% → −9.7% at Re=2000 — the largest gain in the study, exactly where the flow is most OOD.

Both beat the unconditional base. The other two adapters don't: grad_full60 consistently degrades physics (unfreezing the base with the gradient signal is the wrong move), and field_frozen60 sits at roughly no-change everywhere.
